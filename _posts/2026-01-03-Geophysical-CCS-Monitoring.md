### Monitoring CO2 Storage

<div style="text-align: justify"> 
Geophysical monitoring for Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) is a critical, regulatory-driven process that ensures long-term storage safety by tracking CO2 plume migration and detecting potential leakage. It involves analysing changes in subsurface physical properties through methods like 4D seismic imaging, and gravity surveys before, during the three phases of the lifecycle of CO2 storage: 

<dl>
  <dt>Baseline Survey</dt>
  <dd>Conducted before injection to define the initial state of the subsurface.</dd>
  
  <dt>Injection Phase Monitoring:</dt>
  <dd>Frequent surveys to track plume growth and pressure changes to optimize injection strategies.</dd>
  
  <dt>Post-Closure Monitoring:</dt>
  <dd>Continued surveillance to confirm the stability of the storage site, until risks are deemed negligible. </dd>
</dl>

</div> 


### Key Geophysical Technologies for CCS 

<div style="text-align: justify"> 
  
<dl>
  <dt>Time-Lapse (4D) Seismic Surveys:</dt>
  <dd>The primary tool, used to visualize the movement and distribution of the CO2 plume over time.</dd>
  
  <dt>Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) Vertical Seismic Profiling (VSP):</dt>
  <dd>Fiber optic cables placed in wells provide real-time, high-resolution data on temperature and pressure, complementing seismic data.</dd>
  
  <dt>Time-Lapse Gravimetry:</dt>
  <dd>Used to detect density variations caused by the replacing of brine with CO2.</dd>

  <dt>Passive Seismicity Monitoring:</dt>
  <dd>Monitors for microseismic activity to ensure the stability of the geological seal (caprock).</dd>
</dl>

</div>
