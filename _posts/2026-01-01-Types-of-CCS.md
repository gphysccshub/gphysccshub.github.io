Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) and its various spin-offs are a suite of technologies designed to stop CO2 from entering the atmosphere or to actively pull it back out. While they sound like alphabet soup, the distinctions lie in where the carbon comes from and how it is captured.


### Carbon Capture and Storage

<div style="text-align: justify"> 

This is the "baseline" technology. It focuses on capturing emissions at the source—usually large industrial points like fossil fuel power plants, steel mills, or cement factories. Instead of letting flue gas go up the chimney, it’s scrubbed (often using chemical solvents). The captured CO2 is compressed and transported via pipeline to be injected deep underground into geological formations. To make high-emission industries "carbon neutral" or "lower carbon" by preventing new emissions.


</div>

### Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS)

<div style="text-align: justify"> 

BECCS is a carbon removal (negative emissions) technology. It combines renewable energy with carbon storage. Plants and trees grow, naturally absorbing CO2via photosynthesis. This biomass is burned for electricity or processed into biofuels. The CO2 released during combustion is captured (using CCS) and stored underground. Because the plants took CO2 out of the air while growing, and that carbon is now permanently locked underground, the process theoretically results in negative emissions.

</div>

###  Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage (DACCS)

<div style="text-align: justify"> 

Unlike the others, DACCS doesn't need a smokestack or a farm. It pulls $CO_{2}$ directly from the ambient air around us. Massive fans pull air through filters or chemical solutions that bind specifically to CO2. Once the filters are saturated, they are heated or treated to release the pure gas, which is then buried. It can be located anywhere (usually near renewable energy sources or storage sites) and addresses "legacy" emissions already in the atmosphere. It is, however, currently very expensive and energy-intensive.

</div>

###  Waste-to-Energy with Carbon Capture and Storage (WECCS)

<div style="text-align: justify"> 

WECCS is a specialized subset of CCS applied to the waste management sector. Municipal solid waste is incinerated to generate heat or electricity. CCS technology is then applied to the incineration plant’s exhaust. Since a significant portion of trash is "biogenic" (paper, food scraps, wood), WECCS can function similarly to BECCS, potentially achieving net-negative emissions while solving the problem of landfill methane.

</div>
